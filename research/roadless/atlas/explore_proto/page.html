… against conductance added

Permeability is the share of the walking effort the roads remove. Picture every home sending its people out to the street through the gaps between buildings: P is the effort that walk spends, the power the flow dissipates, which is also the homes' total escape time. Permeability is 1 − P(roads) / P(no roads), so 0 is no change and 100% would be no effort at all. Conductance added is how much easier the roads make the ground to cross, in m² of open ground: a road conducts ten times what open ground does.

Why colour and contours, not a 3D height map

A height map shows the escape time as terrain: the higher the ground, the longer the way out. It is the field from the Escape time view at a {{HEIGHT_PCT}}% budget, coloured the same, with the roads traced on the floor.

So the page keeps the flat map and adds isochrones, which carry the magnitude as rings you can count, and puts the totals on the plots, where position along an axis is easier to compare than height or colour.

The runs steepen with size, so the fit under-predicts its largest block ({{TOPO_LARGEST_N}} buildings, {{TOPO_LARGEST_H}} hours). The paper's own procedure keeps the best of many runs, which multiplies the time again.
