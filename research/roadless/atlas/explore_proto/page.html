Stage · Screening

Why this block

A metro has far too many blocks to reblock every one of them, so the pipeline opens with one cheap number per block and a floor on it. The shipped screen is the depth proxy times density, √(nA)/P · n/A, from each block's building count n, area A and perimeter P: a block scores high only if it is both deep, its interior many parcels back from a street, and crowded. Precision and recall are measured against the City of Cape Town's own informal-structure survey. At the shipped floor the pool is {{SC_POOL}} blocks, {{SC_PREC}}% of them informal settlements by that survey, and it holds {{SC_REC}}% of the survey's settlement blocks. The floor is absolute, not a percentile, so it applies the same bar however the corpus grows or shrinks. Screening only decides which blocks are worth the expensive work below.

On the ground

Shack fires reported near the block

The screen reads only building counts and block shapes. News reports from the surrounding area show what a deep, crowded block means when a fire starts. None of these fires is confirmed inside block 5810 itself; each place is stated as the report gives it.

  1. May 2016

    Japhta and Dlayedwa streets, Bloekombos, Kraaifontein. OpenStreetMap puts Dlayedwa Street about 80 m outside the block. marker 2016

    Two shacks were set alight, allegedly with a petrol bomb, and four members of one family died: a woman and three children aged 8 to 13. Neighbours tried to fight it with water and could not.

    Cape Times, 23 May 2016: “Four die in suspected arson attack” ↗

  2. Feb 2020

    Wallacedene, Kraaifontein, beside the block. The report names Mfeketo Street, which could not be placed on the map.

    A fire at about 2am burned 28 shacks, displaced 83 people and killed two. Fire engines could not drive in: a community leader said “we have no roads and the shacks are closely clustered”, so crews parked on Mfeketo Street and walked to the fire. The taps gave no water, and residents pulled down shacks to stop it spreading.

    GroundUp, 25 February 2020: “Couple dies in shack fire in Kraaifontein” ↗

  3. Jun 2022

    Pietersen Road, Wallacedene. OpenStreetMap puts Pietersen Road about 1.2 km south of the block. marker 2022

    Three shacks burned, 11 people were displaced and three siblings died.

    News24, 17 June 2022: “Three siblings die in Cape Town fire that displaced 11 people” ↗

  4. Oct 2024

    Manuel Street, Wallacedene, which could not be placed on the map.

    Four people died, three of them children, in one of 48 shack fires across Cape Town that weekend. JP Smith, the City's mayoral committee member for safety and security, said its crews arrive quickly but are “hampered by other factors such as access”; he spoke of the weekend's fires as a whole.

    TimesLIVE, 28 October 2024: “Nine people perish in weekend informal settlement fires in Cape Town” ↗

  5. Jul 2025

    Maroela Road, Wallacedene. OpenStreetMap puts the Wallacedene stretch of Maroela Road about 630 m south of the block; the road also passes within about 16 m of the block's south edge. marker 2025

    About 20 shacks burned and 60 to 80 people were displaced. A 6-year-old girl died.

    EWN, 15 July 2025 ↗ · Cape Argus, 17 July 2025 ↗

Permeability is the share of the walking effort the roads remove. Picture every home sending its people out to the street through the gaps between buildings: P is the effort that walk spends, the power the flow dissipates, which is also the homes' total escape time. Permeability is 1 − P(roads) / P(no roads), so 0 is no change and 100% would be no effort at all. Conductance added is how much easier the roads make the ground to cross, in m² of open ground: a road conducts ten times what open ground does.

1 An extrapolation, not a measurement. Topology (Brelsford, Martin and Bettencourt) was timed once on each of six smaller Cape Town blocks, at α = 10 on one thread ({{TOPO_RUNS}}). A least-squares power law through all six gives time ∝ buildings{{TOPO_EXP}} ± {{TOPO_SE}}, which reaches about {{TOPO}} at this block's {{HOMES}} buildings. The runs steepen with size, so the fit under-predicts its largest block ({{TOPO_LARGEST_N}} buildings, {{TOPO_LARGEST_H}} hours). Anchored on that run, the five smaller blocks' exponent ({{TOPO_SMALL_EXP}}) gives {{TOPO_LOW}} and the slope between the two largest ({{TOPO_TOP_EXP}}) gives {{TOPO_HIGH}}. The paper's own procedure keeps the best of many runs, which multiplies the time again. Source: the group-Steiner study (docs/superpowers/notes/2026-09-26-topology-is-a-group-steiner-tree.md; its timing script on branch research/group-steiner).
