Every plate uses one encoding. Each home is coloured by its escape time as a share of the 99th-percentile home's with no new road: blue a short way out, red a long one, a line every 10%. New road is dark slate, 7 m wide. A damaged building fades toward sand. Escape time saved is 1 − P(roads) / P(no roads), the site's permeability.

The screen as a height map

Each block is coloured blue below the floor and red above it, passing through pale at the floor, so the edge of the set the screen selects is where the colour turns. Two colourings share that scale. Rank on each side spends half the colours on each side of the floor and spreads each half by area: a block's shade is the share of that side's area scoring below it, so every shade covers as much of the map as any other and the few blocks above the floor get as many shades as the many below. Confidence asks how sure the score is that a block is over the floor. Treating its building count n as Poisson, its log score has standard deviation k/√n, k the score's power of n (3/2 for depth × density, 1 for n/P²: area and perimeter are taken as exact), and the shade is the chance that the true score is above the floor. Most blocks hold enough buildings to sit plainly on one side, so this colouring is nearly two-tone, the pale blocks small and near the floor. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan. The ring marks 5810.

The outlined shapes are the best circles, ellipses, squares and rectangles under one of two objectives. Excess sums each covered pixel's log score above the shipped floor, times its area. A shape grows while the ground it adds is above the floor on balance, so the objective picks its own size. Surprise compares a shape's mean log score with the best mean a shape of the same size reaches by chance, with the blocks' scores shuffled among the blocks (32 shuffles): (mean − the shuffled bests' mean) / their spread. That accounts for how many places a small shape can be put. Raw z skips the shuffle and compares a shape with every other placement of its size. It is kept to show the bias: small shapes win by chance, and here they just find the single top block. Every objective counts a placement only if blocks cover at least 60% of it, and keeps up to three disjoint shapes per family.

The numbered outlines are convex hulls of clusters of whole blocks, found by hull agglomeration: starting from single blocks, it repeatedly merges the two nearby clusters whose union scores best by mean log score plus log(blocks' area / convex hull's area), so a merge across empty ground is penalised. Each cluster it passes through is a candidate. Worse than the city is the upper-level-set scan statistic over those candidates: a likelihood ratio for the cluster's mean log score exceeding the rest of the city's, weighted by buildings, with clusters holding at most half the city's buildings. Its p-value compares that ratio with the largest ratio anywhere in the tree when the scores are shuffled among the blocks. Contrast is the cluster's mean log score minus that of the blocks sharing a street with it.
