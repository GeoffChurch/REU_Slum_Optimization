Clearings, built out side by side

Pick a clearing for each side, then drag the displacement budget: each method clears whole buildings, and builds no road, up to that share of the homes' floor area. SIMP and the cheap preset solve each budget afresh, so a frame is not the last one plus more; the greedy is one build order cut at each budget, the cheap preset that cut polished by swaps. Two more are SIMP with another objective, J₂, which weights each home by its escape time so the far homes count more, and SIMP under another rule for homes walled in at baseline; both are scored here under the page's metric. Switch the view to see the clearing, or the walking flow it creates.

Pick a network for each side, then drag the displacement budget and both grow to it, each cut the way the research lineup cuts it: the shortest prefix of the method's roads that displaces at least that share of the homes' floor area. Switch the view to see the roads, or the walking flow they create. Fifteen networks: cycle_native and clearance_looped as they are, each guided by the walking's power, cycle_native with its loop's two legs kept 7 or 14 m apart, cycle_native choosing its loops by how much they lower P (with the loop's two legs anywhere, or kept 7 or 14 m apart), and ΔP greedy, which picks every road by how much it lowers P: solving four or twelve candidates a step, or exactly, from the lineup's roads alone or with road SIMP's added, each of those two also built by how much it lowers J₂, which weights far homes more. Three more are road SIMP's answers under the adaptive update at γ 0.5, 1 and 1.5: each is one network rather than a build order, so it grows in the lineup's street-first order up to its own displacement and is shown whole beyond it. The last come from the pilots of 27 October. Laplacian growth grows road from the street as a front, a batch of road-width patches a step, either as road at ten times open ground's conductance or as a leak to ground; each step is a prefix, cut at the first step that reaches the budget, and all of them are carved and scored here as ordinary road. Ears grow the same grounded way but a whole road at a time, from one face's edge back to it, so every road closes a loop. The joint SIMP paves and clears together under one price on the conductance added. The partition SIMP reblocks from the top down: it splits the block into labelled sub-blocks, starting from the spectral normalized cut of the buildings, and lays the roads where the labels change, read as the label boundaries (cut), so every road is on a loop, or as the optimizer's own band, which breaks where it thins and leaves dead ends. The grid templates lay parallel or crossing lines through the gaps, its baseline. Their answers exist only at the budgets they were solved for, so they are diamonds on the chart, and between budgets the plates hold the answer below.

    How the power-guided versions differ

    The baseline's flow is solved once, and the ridges of its power, where the walking spends the most effort, become desire lines. Each method routes its candidate roads along them, the way cycle_desire routes along betweenness, but still chooses roads by its own objective. Guidance helps the tree method a great deal: clearance_looped reaches 0.374 at 5% displacement instead of 0.239, and 0.504 at 15% instead of 0.421. It does not help the loop method: cycle_native is 0.366 at 5% instead of 0.392, and 0.511 at 15% instead of 0.575.

    ΔP greedy

    Instead of being drawn toward the power, ΔP greedy picks each road by what it does to P. Every road of the two lineup networks is a candidate, all of them carved into one mesh so any set can be solved exactly. A connected candidate that displaces no one is built at once, since added conductance never raises P. Otherwise the power now dissipated in each candidate's corridor, over its displacement, ranks them (a lower bound on the drop it brings), and the top four are solved exactly. It beats every network above at every budget: 0.438 at 5%, 0.575 at 10% and 0.645 at 15%, against cycle_native's 0.392, 0.520 and 0.575. The step's winner was often fourth by the screen, so a second run solves twelve a step: 0.454, 0.583 and 0.652, a little better again, for 8 hours of compute against 3.7, and its winner was still often the twelfth. The screen ignores the ground a road opens through buildings; a screen that counted it would pay more than solving more.

    ΔP greedy, exact, and under J₂

    The screen can go. The lazy greedy keeps every candidate's last exact drop in P and each step re-solves the five with the best kept drop per displacement until the leader's is fresh; it picks better than either screened run, in less time: 0.495 at 5%, 0.612 at 10% and 0.678 at 15%, in 1.7 hours. Adding road SIMP's roads, at 5% and at 15%, to the candidates lifts it to 0.611, 0.715 and 0.765, the best network here, bought partly with road: 14.7 km at 15% against 9.2.

    Each is also built picking the road that lowers J₂ most per displacement: each home's escape time squared times its people, so a home twice as far counts four times. Roads already reach the far homes in proportion (every network's J₂ lens is within about 0.01 of its LP, where the clearings' trail by 0.02), so there is little to move. On the lineup's candidates the J₂ build is no better even on J₂ (0.665 against 0.670 at 15%): picking one road at a time, the best steps for J₂ do not add up to the best network for it. With SIMP's roads among the candidates it leads on J₂ by up to 0.028 (at 5%) at about the same LP: at 5% the deepest quarter of homes reaches 0.38× its baseline escape time against 0.42×, while the homes nearest the street gain less, and by 10% the deepest are level. The Time view shows both.

    Road SIMP's adaptive answers

    Road SIMP lays road density on a 1 m raster and moves it toward where the walking current runs: the adaptive transport network's rule, density growing as the current to the power 2/(1+γ) under a paving budget priced as area to the power γ. Below γ = 1 wide trunks are cheap and the answer is a tree; above it they are dear and the flow spreads over parallel paths and loops. Here at a 5% budget, 0.01 of LP per km, with U's clearing as the vote's device. The pictures are each answer's paved ground; the networks above are its skeleton as 7 m roads, kept where it reaches the street, so they stop at 1.6 to 3.7% displacement and lose what the wide paving bought (paved LP 0.73 to 0.76). ΔP greedy reaches 0.31 to 0.44 at the same displacements with 1.5 to 2.5 km.

    cycle_native scored by ΔP, and its loops kept wide

    This keeps cycle_native's own move, a loop out to one of the eight deepest unserved homes and back, and chooses the loop to build by the exact drop in P per displacement instead of the older permeability. Its roads crowd like ΔP greedy's. So do plain cycle_native's, cut in the order it built them. The way back avoids only the way out's own edges, so in a tight gap it takes the next edge over and the loop is one road laid twice. Most of cycle_native's loops are like that, ΔP-scored or not.

    The lineup's cut hides this. It orders a network built on to 37% displacement street first, then cuts that, which leaves out one leg of many narrow loops: at 15%, 9% of the road lies within 5 m of another, against 26% in build order. It also scores higher: 0.520 at 10%, against 0.479. The ΔP runs stop at 15% and are cut in build order, so they compare with plain cycle_native's build order. ΔP lifts it from 0.384, 0.479 and 0.571 at 5%, 10% and 15% to 0.405, 0.521 and 0.592.

    Keeping the way back at least 7 m from the way out, one road width, removes the crowding in both: at most 1% of the road lies within 5 m of another at any budget. It costs P. Plain cycle_native, cut the lineup's way, falls from 0.392, 0.520 and 0.575 to 0.359, 0.462 and 0.554. Scored by ΔP, in build order, it reaches 0.388, 0.496 and 0.570. At 14 m apart, plain cycle_native scores 0.385, 0.470 and 0.545, and ΔP-scored 0.416, 0.489 and 0.565.

    Read before comparing roads and clearing

    Every picture and score here uses the current metric, which gives a street ten times the conductance of open ground only where it is tagged paved, and gives every new road the same ten times. Almost none of 5810's own lanes are paved, so a new road along a lane counts as paving it. That is why the networks remove far more of P than the research lineup recorded under the older metric (cycle_native 0.392 at 5%, against 0.050), and why they now beat SIMP's clearing (0.235), which opens ground but paves nothing. Left unboosted, cycle_native and clearance_looped score 0.080 and 0.057 at 5%.

    How the four clearings compare

    SIMP leads the cheap preset at every budget, by 0.007 to 0.021: 0.255 against 0.246 at 5%, 0.346 against 0.337 at 10% and 0.401 against 0.393 at 15%. The polish lifts the greedy by 0.009 and 0.018 at 1% and 2%, and by at most 0.004 above that, where it stops after a few swaps. The greedy's own cut at 2% stops at 1.4%: its second batch overshoots the budget, and the cut leaves out the batch's large buildings. SIMP optimized under the rule that counts every walled-in home's trip ends within 0.001 of the default under this page's metric, ahead at 13 of the 15 budgets. Every clearing frees 5810's two walled-in homes, and each method's LP rises with every step of the budget.

    Does it clear further in? J₂, and the Time view

    Every clearing here takes the middle, not the edge: ranked by how long its people take to reach the street with nothing cleared, the median cleared floor area sits at 0.36 to 0.44 of the way from the shortest trips to the longest, with only 6 to 14% of it among the quarter of homes nearest the street and 0 to 5% among the farthest quarter. These are the bottlenecks between the deep homes and the exits. The Time view shows where the gain lands: homes beside the cleared band take half the time or less, and the farthest quarter of homes a median 0.81× at 5% and 0.67× at 15%, a gain the current and power views barely show.

    J₂ squares each home's escape time, so a home twice as far counts four times. It clears a little further in: a median of 0.41 to 0.49 of the way, 9% among the farthest quarter at 15% against SIMP's 5%. The farthest quarter's median escape time falls to 0.659× its own at 15%, against 0.672× under P, while the middle half's falls less (0.642× against 0.624×). It gives up 0.002 to 0.004 of LP for 0.002 to 0.003 of LJ₂: on this block the objective moves the clearing only slightly.

    Clearings against roads

    Under this metric a new road counts as paved, ten times the conductance of open ground, while a clearing opens ground and paves nothing. So the best clearing removes far less of P than the roads: SIMP's 0.255 at 5%, against cycle_native's 0.392 and ΔP greedy's 0.454; 0.401 at 15%, against 0.575 and 0.652. The ordering turns on the boost: left unboosted, cycle_native and clearance_looped score 0.080 and 0.057 at 5%, under a third of any clearing there. SIMP's clearing at 5%, optimized under this metric, removes 0.255, against 0.235 for the older clearing marked on the Roads chart.
