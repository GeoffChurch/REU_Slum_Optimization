# Route (A): the LP, and the currency the greedy was budgeting wrong

The greedy (`resistance_greedy`) beat clearance on only 6/10 blocks, and pushing on search made it
*worse* -- shortlist 20 scored 0.6622 against shortlist 6's 0.6752. That falsified "the gap is
search quality" and pointed at greedy myopia, which is exactly what route (A) fixes: optimize the
whole road set at once instead of one road at a time.

The design note shelved route (A) on rounding: total effective resistance is convex in edge
conductances (Ghosh-Boyd-Saberi), but "the ROUNDING is the hard part, because a road is a
combinatorial frontage PATH, not a free edge."

## Getting around the rounding: never let edges be the decision variables

    x_p in [0,1]   candidate road p -- a traced Dijkstra path from a parcel back to the network
    z_s in [0,1]   substrate segment s is built
    y_e in [0,1]   parcel-adjacency edge e is upgraded from footpath to road
    u_b in [0,1]   building b is displaced

    max  sum_e g_e y_e                        g_e = dg_e (v_i - v_j)^2, the linearized gain
    s.t. sum_b u_b   <= D * n_buildings       the lens's own budget
         sum_s len_s z_s <= B_m               buildability cap
         x_p <= z_s           for s in p      choosing a path builds every segment of it
         z_s <= sum_{p ni s} x_p              a segment exists only because some path wants it
         y_e <= sum_{s ni e} z_s              an adjacency edge upgrades only if a road covers it
         c_b(s) z_s <= u_b    for s near b    displacement is a MAX over segments, not a sum

Deciding over paths rather than edges makes connectivity structural instead of a rounding repair:
`x_p <= z_s` means any solution with `x_p > 0` has bought the whole path back to the street, and
`z_s <= sum x_p` closes the converse -- without it the LP sets `z_s = 1` on high-gain segments with
every `x_p = 0`, buying gain without buying a road, which is the fractional nonsense route (A) was
shelved for. Rounding then commits paths in LP-confidence order, street-first, so a committed
prefix always reaches the street. Measured: 98.8% of road length is connected to the street.

## The finding that mattered was not the LP, it was the budget

The first version budgeted METRES and lost badly -- 1/6 against clearance. The diagnostic
(`lp_diag.py`) split the failure three ways and the third number gave it away: an *unconstrained*
per-metre greedy on the same linearized gain scored **1.37-1.65x the LP optimum** and reached
P = 0.98, which is impossible if the LP were relaxing the same problem. It wasn't -- that greedy
was buying floating segments, and its displacement was **0.27-0.38**, three to four times the
budget.

Which exposed the actual error: **both lenses score displacement, and metres are a bad proxy for
it.** The same metres cost wildly different displacement depending on whether they run down a gap
or through the dense interior. The metre-budgeted LP sat at D = 0.080-0.118 where clearance sat at
0.060-0.096, and lost the lens on that alone.

Budgeting displacement directly is something an LP can do and a greedy cannot. `budget.displacement`
measures each building against the UNION corridor, so its cost is `max_s c_b(s)`, not `sum_s`; the
`u_b` family is exactly that max, and two roads flanking one building pay for it once. Marginal
displacement is not CELF-safe -- which is precisely why `greedy_arterial` had to abandon it for
`repulsion`. As a linear constraint it costs nothing.

**But Lens A leaves road length unbudgeted, and the LP spends 3.7x the metres.** Capped at

### Granularity control: the win survives, smaller

segments can stop closer to the target than one emitting whole paths. The LP emits segments;

## `chunks` calibrated against lens B

It had been fixed at 4 from one 6-block LENS A sweep in which k=16 actually scored higher
(0.868 vs 0.826) -- the carried-forward value was not even that sweep's winner. Swept properly
against lens B (n = 12, displacement to reach P* = 0.60, lower better):

| chunks | disp to reach P* | metres | secs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0646 | 113 | 0.09 |
| 2 | 0.0635 | 144 | 0.17 |
| 4 | 0.0460 | 173 | 0.36 |
| **8** | **0.0397** | 197 | 0.60 |
| 16 | 0.0395 | 222 | 1.19 |
| 32 | 0.0373 | 203 | 2.45 |

Monotone with a plateau: k=8 beats k=4 on 8/12 (median -0.0058), k=16 beats k=4 on only 7/12 for
twice the cost, k=32 on 7/11. **Default set to 8**, where the curve flattens -- still 6x faster
than `resistance_greedy` and 65x faster than arterial. This is the shape the theory predicts (more
re-linearization, less linearization error, diminishing returns), which is the point of it not
being a fitted knob.


## Does the refocusing generalize? Yes -- and it is worth about a third of the win

If the LP wins because it budgets displacement rather than metres, then simply re-denominating an
existing method's cost in displaced homes should capture most of it without any LP. `greedy_drainage`
says "the cost field is the ONLY thing that varies between reblockers built on this", so clearance
can be re-denominated with no change to src. Three cost fields on one base, lens B, n = 12:

| clearance cost field | disp to reach P* | metres | secs | vs shipped repulsion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pure length | 0.1018 | 71 | 0.03 | 0/12, +0.0155, p=0.0005 |
| **repulsion (shipped)** | 0.0861 | 75 | 0.03 | -- |
| **displacement** | **0.0765** | 112 | 0.14 | **10/12, -0.0145, p=0.012** |
| (LP, for scale) | 0.0397 | 197 | 0.65 | 12/12, -0.0465, p=0.0005 |

A clean ordering: **length < repulsion < displacement < LP**. Two things follow.

1. **Repulsion is doing real work.** Pure length is significantly WORSE than repulsion (0/12,
   p = 0.0005), so the soft proximity proxy is not decoration -- it is most of why clearance is not
   terrible. Denominating in the scored quantity itself beats the proxy, but only by a little.
2. **The currency is about a third of the story, not all of it.** Refocusing buys -0.0145 of the
   LP's -0.0465 -- roughly 31%. The remaining ~69% is the set-level formulation: seeing every road
   at once, crediting shared trunks once, and charging a building's displacement once no matter how
   many roads graze it. A greedy cannot do any of those at any price, because marginal displacement
   is not CELF-safe.

This corrects an overstatement earlier in this note's history: "the LP only wins because it
optimizes the currency that's actually scored" is wrong. The currency is necessary -- the
metre-budgeted LP lost outright -- but it is a minority of the advantage.

**Cheap actionable finding:** clearance's shipped repulsion cost can be swapped for a displacement
cost for a significant lens-B improvement at ~4x its (negligible) cost, independent of anything
about the LP. Not yet done -- it is a change to a shipped flagship, measured at `depth_target=1` on
12 blocks, and deserves its own confirmation at the example configuration first.


## Region scale broke it, and the break is partly the LENS, not the method

Adding `resistance_lp` to the two multiblock examples produced two unusable pictures and one real
bug that predates the LP.

### The LP exploits the unbudgeted length axis (real, LP-specific)

On the `depth` example, lens A: permeability **0.955** at D = 0.10, bought with **42,937 m** of road
against `clearance_looped`'s 9,878 m. The picture is a capillary web paving nearly every parcel
boundary. That region is sparse, so parcel-boundary roads cost almost no displacement, and
"maximize permeability subject to displacement <= D" then has no reason to stop. It is not a road
network, it is a mesh.

The evidence for this had been in hand and under-weighted: `lp_k4_matchlen`, capped at clearance's
metres, lost to every method at 1-2/10. **Lens A does not constrain road length, and every greedy
method hid that by construction.** An actual optimizer does not.

### The disconnection was MY INSTRUMENT. Retracted.

I reported that lens-B prefixes were badly disconnected -- LP 0.555, resistance_greedy 0.581,
arterial 0.745 -- and concluded the lens had been scoring unbuildable road sets for three of five
methods. **That was a measurement bug, not a finding.**

`connected_fraction` built its graph with `budget._noded_graph`, which runs `unary_union` first.
That planarizes and re-nodes the geometry into vertices that no longer match the `_rnd`-snapped
keys the membership test looks for, so it reported spurious disconnection.

`clearance` caught it. It builds a drainage tree in which every road is a Dijkstra path terminating
at the street, so its connectivity is **1.000 by construction** -- an available known answer that
the instrument failed. Rebuilt on the raw-coordinate graph exactly as `road_drainage` builds it:

| method | full network (n=10 blocks) | | region-scale lens-B prefix |
|---|---|---|---|
| | median | min | |
| clearance | **1.000** | **1.000** | 0.973 (as `clearance_looped`) |
| resistance_greedy | 0.949 | 0.784 | -- |
| resistance_lp | **1.000** | 0.974 | **1.000** |

Re-measured at block scale, the lens-B prefix ranking is the **reverse** of what I claimed -- the LP
is the best method on this axis, not the worst:

| method | disp to reach P* | prefix connected | blocks < 0.99 | min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| clearance | 0.1018 | 1.000 | 2/12 | 0.780 |
| clearance (repulsion) | 0.1026 | 1.000 | 1/12 | 0.697 |
| greedy_arterial_repulsion | 0.1161 | 0.782 | 9/12 | 0.437 |
| resistance_greedy | 0.1044 | 0.900 | 6/12 | 0.000 |
| **resistance_lp** | **0.0374** | **1.000** | **0/12** | **1.000** |

So the LP's connectivity IS structural, as the `x_p <= z_s` constraint claims. A **milder** version
of the lens concern is real and worth keeping: arterial (0.782 median) and `resistance_greedy`
(0.900, min 0.000) do produce genuinely fragmented scored prefixes, and even clearance slips on
2/12 -- a later Dijkstra path can branch off the middle of an earlier one, so drainage-descending
is not always a topological order even for a tree. That is a real if modest defect in
`_drainage_ordered`, affecting the two loop-bearing greedies and not the LP.

The lesson is the cheap one I skipped: **`clearance` is a known-answer oracle for any road-set
property that a drainage tree satisfies by construction.** Running the instrument against it first
would have caught this before it produced three wrong conclusions.

### What actually survives

**The 43 km capillary web is real and is the LP's genuine defect.** It is measured in metres, so no
connectivity instrument touches it: 42,937 m against `clearance_looped`'s 9,878 m on the depth
example, for permeability 0.955 at D = 0.10. Lens A budgets displacement and not length; on a sparse
region parcel-boundary roads cost almost no displacement, so the optimizer has no reason to stop.
`lp_k4_matchlen` -- the same LP capped at clearance's metres -- lost to every method at 1-2/10,
which was the evidence in hand that I under-weighted.

**The lens-B result stands, and is not contaminated.** Under the repo's canonical
`prefix_to_permeability` the LP reaches P* at 0.0374 displacement against clearance's 0.1018, with a
fully connected prefix.

So the correct verdict on route (A) is narrower than my first claim and much narrower than my
retraction:

- Lens B (displacement to reach a permeability target): the LP wins, connected, real.
- Lens A (permeability at a displacement cap): the win is an artifact of the unbudgeted length axis,
  and lens A needs a length budget before any method is compared on it.

### The length price: the metre problem is SPARSE-FABRIC-SPECIFIC, and the price does not fix it

Lens A caps displacement and says nothing about metres, so `length_price` was added to the objective
(normalized per instance by its own best gain-per-metre, so one value means the same thing
everywhere) rather than a hard cap -- a cap has to be tuned per block, and tuning it to match a
baseline begs the question the comparison asks.

Swept at pinned P* = 0.60, n = 10 blocks, reporting BOTH cost axes:

| method | disp to reach P* | prefix m | full network m | prefix connected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| clearance | 0.0969 | 81 | 415 | 1.000 |
| clearance (repulsion) | 0.0673 | 104 | 430 | 1.000 |
| greedy_arterial_repulsion | 0.1327 | 153 | 697 | 0.947 |
| resistance_greedy | 0.1077 | 64 | 571 | 0.866 |
| lp, price 0 | **0.0271** | 98 | **333** | 1.000 |
| lp, price 1 | 0.0337 | 92 | 334 | 1.000 |
| lp, price 4 | 0.0325 | 156 | 346 | 1.000 |

| vs clearance | disp better | metres better | **BOTH** |
|---|---|---|---|
| lp, price 0 | 9/9 | 1/9 | 1/9 |
| lp, price 1 | 9/9 | 0/9 | 0/9 |
| lp, price 2 | 10/10 | 2/10 | 2/10 |
| lp, price 4 | 8/9 | 1/9 | 1/9 |

Two results, both negative for the price and one surprising:

1. **No price dominates.** The LP wins displacement at every price (8-10/10) and metres almost never
   (0-2/10). This is a **Pareto trade, not a win**: 2.7x fewer homes displaced for ~15% more metres
   in the scored prefix.
2. **The price is a pure loss at block scale.** Raising it makes displacement worse (0.0271 ->
   0.0354) without buying fewer metres -- price 4 uses MORE prefix metres (156) than price 0 (98).

The reason is in the `full network m` column, and it inverts the diagnosis: **at block scale the
LP's whole network is already 22% SHORTER than clearance's** (333 m vs 415 m), and shorter than
every other method. There is no general metre problem. The capillary web is specific to **sparse
fabric**, where a 10%-displacement budget buys an enormous number of gap-running metres and only an
optimizer notices. So the honest framing is not "the LP over-builds" but "on sparse fabric D = 0.10
is a very loose constraint".

## Final status

What survives, stated at the width the evidence supports:

- **Lens B: the LP wins, robustly.** 2.7x less displacement to reach P* = 0.60 (0.0271 vs 0.0969),
  best-in-class prefix connectivity (1.000 on 12/12 blocks), full network shorter than every
  baseline. Route (A) works, and the rounding obstacle really does dissolve when the decision
  variables are paths.
- **It is a Pareto improvement, not domination.** The cost is ~15% more metres in the scored prefix.
- **Lens A should not be used to compare anything** until it budgets road length. The LP exposed
  that, but the hole belongs to the lens, and a length price on the method does not patch it.
### `length_price` failed and was deleted

Tested where the web actually appeared -- the sparse `depth` region, 11,006 parcels:

| method | full m | lens A m | lens A P | lens B m | lens B D | lens B conn | secs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clearance_looped | 18,061 | 9,878 | 0.7828 | 2,804 | 0.0254 | 0.831 | 36 |
| lp, price 0 | 43,183 | 42,937 | 0.9549 | **1,951** | **0.0066** | **1.000** | 289 |
| lp, price 1 | 42,296 | 42,013 | 0.9508 | 1,925 | 0.0077 | 1.000 | 630 |
| lp, price 4 | 39,386 | 39,183 | 0.9467 | 1,867 | 0.0083 | 1.000 | 478 |
| lp, price 16 | 33,623 | 33,366 | 0.9300 | 1,965 | 0.0148 | 1.000 | 147 |

The price moves metres monotonically but far too weakly: at price 16 it still draws 33,623 m against
18,061 m, having made displacement 2.2x worse (0.0066 -> 0.0148). Combined with being a pure loss at
block scale, that makes it an option nobody would ever select -- deleted under the repo's own rule
rather than left as a knob. `max_road_m` (a hard cap, off by default) remains.

Why a price cannot work here: on sparse fabric a gap road genuinely IS nearly free in displacement
and genuinely DOES raise permeability, so the LP is right by the objective it was given. What is
missing is a construction cost, which no shipped metric represents. That is lens A's gap, not a
method's.

### The best result in the investigation is on this same region

Lens B, depth region: the LP reaches P* = 0.60 with **1,951 m and 0.66% displacement** against
`clearance_looped`'s **2,804 m and 2.54%** -- 3.8x fewer homes displaced AND 30% fewer metres, with
a fully connected prefix where clearance_looped's is 0.831. **That is domination on both axes**, not
a Pareto trade.

So the block-scale metre deficit does not reproduce at region scale. The two regions differ:
`depth` dominates on both axes; `density_compactness` is a Pareto trade (2,236 m at 0.0403 vs
1,474 m at 0.0564). Region scale is where this method is meant to be used, and it is where it looks
best.

## Final status

- **Lens B is a robust LP win** -- 2.7x less displacement at block scale, 3.8x less AND fewer metres
  on the depth region, best-in-class prefix connectivity (12/12 blocks at 1.000). Route (A) works;
  the rounding obstacle dissolves when the decision variables are paths.
- **Lens A cannot compare anything until it budgets road length.** The LP exposed the hole; the hole
  belongs to the lens. A method-side price does not patch it -- measured, not assumed.
- **Cost**: ~290 s on an 11,000-parcel region, versus 36 s for the flagship.

Ready to ship on lens B. Held out of the published examples only because the example generator
reports lens A too, and that picture is a 43 km mesh until lens A gains a length budget.


## The prefix-ordering fix, and what it costs the LP's headline

`_drainage_ordered` was replaced by `_street_first_ordered` (network distance to the street
ascending, ties by drainage descending), so every scored prefix is a connected network by
construction. Measured on the same 12 blocks, before and after:

| method | prefix connected before | after | disp to reach P* before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| clearance | 1.000 (2/12 bad) | 1.000 (0/12) | 0.1018 | 0.0924 |
| clearance (repulsion) | 1.000 (1/12 bad) | 1.000 (0/12) | 0.1026 | 0.1009 |
| greedy_arterial_repulsion | 0.782 (9/12 bad) | 1.000 (0/12) | 0.1161 | **0.1421** |
| resistance_greedy | 0.900 (6/12 bad) | 1.000 (0/12) | 0.1044 | 0.0968 |
| resistance_lp | 1.000 | 1.000 | **0.0374** | **0.0570** |

Every method is now at 1.000 on 12/12 blocks, minimum 1.000.

The fix was not cosmetic, and it moves results in BOTH directions. Arterial gets materially worse
(0.116 -> 0.142), which is the expected sign: it had been credited for fragments it never connected
and now pays for the connectors.

**And it costs the LP part of its margin.** 0.0374 -> 0.0570 against clearance's 0.0924, so the
block-scale advantage is **1.6x, not the 2.7x reported earlier in this note**. The LP's full networks
were always connected -- that part of the earlier retraction stands -- but the *cheapest prefix
reaching P\** had been allowed to skip ahead to high-drainage segments without buying their
connectors. So some of the margin was an ordering artifact after all, just not the one I first
accused it of.

Still the best of the five methods on the lens that matters, still real, materially smaller. Every
lens-B number in this note that predates this section is superseded by it; the region-scale depth
result (1,951 m at 0.66% vs 2,804 m at 2.54%) has not yet been re-measured under the new order and
should be treated as provisional.

## Lens framing (options A and C)

Matched permeability is now the **primary** lens in the generated example READMEs and matched
displacement the **secondary** one, with the length caveat stated in the secondary table's own copy.
Rationale: matched permeability pins the benefit and reports both costs in their own units, so no
exchange rate between homes and metres is needed. A composite `homes + lambda*metres` budget (option
B) was deliberately NOT built -- lambda is a values question, not a measurement one, and inventing a
number risks it hardening into a constant nobody revisits.

Consequence: the committed examples are stale, since every method's prefixes shift.
