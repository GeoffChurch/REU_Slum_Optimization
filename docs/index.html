Rebuilding access, one block at a time.

We generate and evaluate road-network proposals for informal urban settlements — comparing methods that trade off accessibility, cost, and buildability across real slum-block data from Cape Town and Djibouti.

Site in progress. You're looking at an early placeholder linked from our poster QR code. Full method write-ups, before/after visuals, and interactive comparisons are coming soon.

The problem

Over a billion people live in informal settlements without direct access to roads — which means no direct access to water, sanitation, or emergency services either. Reblocking proposes the least-disruptive new roads needed to connect every parcel to the existing street network.

Methods compared

Every method proposes a different road network for the same block. We evaluate all of them on accessibility improvement, road cost, and how buildable the result actually is.

Featured result: Euclidean Grid

A grid-based method inspired by Manhattan-style city planning. Road spacing adapts to local parcel density and hugs real parcel geometry — trading a small amount of raw accessibility for a single, fully-connected, buildable road network.

Peel closes access "k" the most, but fragments the road network into 109 disconnected stubs. The adaptive grid recovers most of that improvement while keeping everything as one connected, buildable network. Tested on 3 real blocks (Cape Town, 2× Djibouti) and validated at scale on an 11,000+ parcel region.
